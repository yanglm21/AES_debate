[[65]]: The essay makes a good attempt at arguing the positive effects of computers, but loses cohesiveness and relevance at parts. The language usage and tone, though casual, are generally appropriate for a letter to a local newspaper, but need some refinement.
------******
CONTENT IMPROVEMENTS:
The writer should work on providing more grounded and diverse arguments. Arguments like "our clothes would be hand-sewn" if there were no computers seems quite stretched and weak. The point about computers preventing boredom is more of a personal anecdote rather than a strong argument. The essay would benefit from more substantial and universal arguments like online learning, accessibility to information, or the opportunities technology provides for global connectivity. 

LANGUAGE IMPROVEMENTS:
The language in the essay is too informal and occasionally veers towards colloquial usage. Phrases like "stomp our feet and clap our hands to make music" or "computers will save me from boredom", while aiming to be illustrative, end up sounding too casual for an opinion piece in a newspaper. The writer should improve the grammar, specifically in terms of article usage ("positive affects", "a part of being a kid") and subject-verb agreement ("computers... is the most positive effect"). There are also some typographical errors like "@CAPS1,@CAPS2 @CAP_STATE3" which should be avoided.

STRUCTURE IMPROVEMENTS:
The essay is divided into small paragraphs, each discussing one point but these points often feel disjointed. The writer should work on transitions between paragraphs to build a more flowing argument. The use of examples, real-life scenarios, or supporting evidence should be incorporated more cohesively into the paragraph structure. A more succinct and compelling conclusion reinforcing the essay's central arguments would also enhance this piece.
