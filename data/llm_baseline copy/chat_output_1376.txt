CONTENT IMPROVEMENTS:
- You may make your points more compelling through the use of statistics or evidence from research to back up your points about health, environmental impact, and interpersonal relationships. 
- Try to expand on your ideas, for example you could explain how the lack of exercise can lead to diseases such as obesity or heart diseases.
- Avoid sounding judgemental. Saying "sitting all day on the computer is not exercise. It is laziness" may offend your readers and make them less receptive to your arguments. Practicing empathy will help your argument resonate better.
 
LANGUAGE IMPROVEMENTS:
- Correct your spelling. For example, 'negitive' should be 'negative', 'exersize' should be 'exercise'. 
- Make sure your verb tenses are consistent. In the same sentence, you have written "You might argue..." and "don't you want to have...".
- Use appropriate punctuation. You are in need of more commas and periods to break up your ideas and make them easier to read and understand.
- Check your sentence structure, as some sentences make no sense, such as "I hope that you agree with my reasons so we can find a way to get everyone their computers and get active."

STRUCTURE IMPROVEMENTS:
- Organize your essay into clear paragraphs to make sure your ideas flow well and your argument is easy to understand.
- You could benefit from an introduction paragraph where you state your argument and then explain what aspects you will discuss. This would provide a clear roadmap for your reader.
- After presenting each point, provide a brief conclusion to recap and further emphasize your points. 
- Try to create a compelling conclusion paragraph that summarizes your arguments and presents a clear final statement. The current ending is rather abrupt and does not really provide closure to the arguments you've made.
