**Censorship in the Libraries**  

Recently, the growing issue of censorship in libraries came to my attention. This is unfortunate in the extreme. People have a right to unmolested knowledge, and censoring libraries is a direct violation of that right. Libraries are meant to be a place of learning, and they should be kept that way.  

History is filled with moments both glorious and inglorious. We aren’t always proud of what we do or what happens, but those events remain, irrespective of whether we choose to acknowledge them. If we cannot erase history’s stains, why do we imagine we can sanitize the present for our children? I realize there are things children should not see or hear, and I’m not suggesting we expose them to these things. I’m merely saying that trying to hide them from what we deem unclean is neither right nor practical. We can’t shelter our children forever; sooner or later, they will learn things we wish they didn’t. This is inevitable. Life doesn’t hide its darker side from us, and to hide it from our children will only handicap them.  

Censorship assumes the arrogant role of arbiter over what minds can handle.
