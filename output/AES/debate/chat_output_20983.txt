### **Final Essay: "The Language of Laughter"**  

Laughter isn’t just sound—it’s the glue that repairs strained connections. In my father’s family, scattered from Texas to Chicago, it’s our shared language. The Texas crew trades barbs like currency—the more outrageous, the richer the affection. For us, jokes are heirlooms, passed down through generations.  

But nothing tests this family language like reunions—especially the spring I became the lone man at my sister’s baby shower, staring down an afternoon of pastel cupcakes and my first encounter with Aunt Margo in a year. Being the only male attendee was daunting enough, but sitting beside her after twelve months of silence? I braced for awkwardness.  

Pastel cupcakes. My aunt’s wheezing laugh—the same one that used to make my childhood soda come out my nose—cut through my nerves instantly. *"Look at you,"* she grinned, *"a rose among thorns."* Soon we were crying over jokes about diaper genies, our shoulders shaking in unison. By the time we battled through the "guess the baby food" game, we’d rewritten a year’s absence into irrelevance.  

As we laughed till our ribs ached that day, I realized our jokes weren’t just heirlooms—they were lifelines, pulling us back to each other across time and distance.
