[[75]]: The essay presents a well-researched and structured argument on the role of immigrant workers in the Canadian economy, but it suffers from some organizational and linguistic inconsistencies that detract from its overall clarity and impact.  
------******  
**CONTENT IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Thesis Clarity:** The introduction hints at the controversy around immigration but does not clearly state the essay’s central argument until later. A stronger, more concise thesis statement upfront would improve coherence.  
2. **Evidence Balance:** While the essay cites multiple sources, some claims (e.g., immigrants’ abuse of social benefits) lack nuanced evidence. Adding specific data or counterarguments would strengthen credibility.  
3. **Depth of Analysis:** The discussion of the Heckscher–Ohlin model is useful but could be more tightly linked to the Canadian context. Explain how the model’s predictions manifest in Canada’s immigration policies or labor market.  
4. **Hypothesis Refinement:** The hypothesis ("immigration is an essential part of the Canadian economy") is broad. Narrow it to a testable claim (e.g., "Immigrant labor boosts GDP growth in sectors X and Y").  

**LANGUAGE IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Wordiness:** Phrases like "obviously one of the most controversial issues" or "as a rule, triggers a range of negative economic consequences" are vague. Replace with precise language (e.g., "Immigration remains contentious due to its perceived economic trade-offs").  
2. **Awkward Phrasing:** Sentences like "the positive effects... are also to be born in mind" are clunky. Revise for fluency ("However, immigration’s positive economic effects warrant equal consideration").  
3. **Inconsistent Terminology:** Alternate terms like "migrant workers," "immigrants," and "infiltrators" risk confusion. Standardize to "immigrant workers" unless distinguishing legal/illegal status.  
4. **Passive Voice:** Overuse (e.g., "the surveys consist of...") weakens impact. Use active voice ("The surveys include...").  

**STRUCTURE IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Section Flow:** The "Problem Statement" and "Theoretical Framework" sections feel disjointed. Merge them into a cohesive "Background" section to contextualize the H-O model’s relevance earlier.  
2. **Methodology Clarity:** The survey design is described twice (under "Local Authorities as Respondents" and "Design of Surveys"). Consolidate for brevity and clarity.  
3. **Conclusion Expansion:** The conclusion abruptly restates the thesis without synthesizing key findings. Summarize results (e.g., "Data confirms immigrant labor’s net GDP contribution outweighs social costs") and propose policy steps.  
4. **Subheadings:** Some subheadings (e.g., "Immigrants as a Cheap Labor Force") are overly simplistic. Use analytical titles (e.g., "Economic Contributions of Low-Wage Immigrant Labor").
