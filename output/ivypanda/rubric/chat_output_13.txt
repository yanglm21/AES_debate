[[75]]: The essay provides a comprehensive overview of key considerations when developing a vendor contract, but it lacks depth in analysis and originality, and the language could be more precise.  
------******  
**CONTENT IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Deeper Analysis**: The essay lists elements of a contract but rarely delves into *why* these elements matter or how they interact. For example, how does shared vision *directly* reduce conflicts? Adding cause-effect analysis would strengthen the argument.  
2. **Original Insights**: Much of the content relies heavily on cited sources (e.g., Vitasek 2011). The writer should synthesize ideas more critically—e.g., contrasting viewpoints on employee displacement or debating the ethics of wage disparities in outsourcing.  
3. **Examples/Case Studies**: Concrete examples (e.g., a failed outsourcing contract due to poor termination clauses) would make the discussion more engaging and practical.  

**LANGUAGE IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Clarity**: Avoid vague phrasing like “appropriate policies will lead to success” (what policies? what metrics define success?). Replace with specifics: *“Policies such as quarterly performance reviews and penalty clauses for missed deadlines ensure accountability.”*  
2. **Conciseness**: Trim redundant phrases (e.g., *“The contract should make note of the expectations and be sensitive to what is happening in the external market”* → *“The contract should align with market trends and clarify expectations.”*).  
3. **Grammar/Flow**: Fix minor errors (e.g., *“consider analyze the management”* → *“consider analyzing the management”*) and use transitions (*“Furthermore,” “Conversely”*) to improve flow between sections.  

**STRUCTURE IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Thesis Statement**: The introduction should clearly state the essay’s central argument (e.g., *“A successful vendor contract balances legal safeguards, mutual goals, and adaptability to market changes”*).  
2. **Subheadings**: The “Elements” section is overly long. Break it into thematic subsections (e.g., *“Legal Protections,” “Operational Efficiency,” “Human Resource Considerations”*) for readability.  
3. **Conclusion**: Expand beyond summary. Reinforce the stakes (e.g., *“Without clear termination clauses, companies risk costly legal battles”*) or propose future trends (e.g., AI’s impact on outsourcing contracts).
