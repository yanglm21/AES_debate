------******  
**CONTENT IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Depth of Analysis**: The essay explains the HR manager’s role well but could delve deeper into *how* specific HR practices (e.g., training, rewards) directly improve product/service quality. For example, include a case study or data to show the impact of HR-led quality initiatives.  
2. **Originality**: The arguments are somewhat generic. Adding unique examples (e.g., a company where HR-driven quality programs succeeded) would strengthen the essay.  
3. **References**: While sources are cited, their integration is superficial. Direct quotes or paraphrased evidence would bolster credibility (e.g., "As Walker (1998) notes, HR’s training programs reduce defects by X%").  

**LANGUAGE IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Clarity**: Some sentences are wordy (e.g., "This can be the basis of offering training..."). Simplify: "Training helps employees view colleagues as internal customers."  
2. **Precision**: Replace vague phrases like "many ways" with specifics: "The HR manager improves quality through three key methods: training, rewards, and..."  
3. **Grammar/Flow**: Fix minor errors (e.g., "lead" → "led" in paragraph 2) and use transitions (e.g., "Furthermore," "Consequently") to connect ideas smoothly.  

**STRUCTURE IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Thesis Statement**: The introduction should explicitly state the essay’s focus (e.g., "This essay argues that HR managers ensure quality by X, Y, and Z").  
2. **Paragraph Unity**: Some paragraphs (e.g., the one starting "The HR manager builds trust...") mix ideas. Split into two: one on trust-building, another on role-modeling.  
3. **Conclusion**: Restate key points more succinctly and avoid new ideas (e.g., remove "necessary conditions" unless elaborated earlier).
