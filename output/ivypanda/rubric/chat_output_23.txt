------******  
**CONTENT IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Depth of Analysis**: The essay outlines the HR manager’s responsibilities well but could delve deeper into specific examples or case studies to illustrate how these roles directly impact product/service quality. For instance, how does training translate to fewer defects in production?  
2. **Critical Engagement**: The argument would benefit from addressing potential counterarguments or challenges HR managers face in implementing total quality management (e.g., resistance to change, budget constraints).  
3. **Updated References**: Some sources (e.g., Walker, 1998; Yuki, 1994) are outdated. Replace with recent studies to strengthen credibility and relevance.  

**LANGUAGE IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Clarity and Precision**: Avoid vague phrases like *“an extent of supervising”* (→ *“extending to supervising”*) and *“This can be the basis of offering training”* (→ *“This justifies training initiatives”*).  
2. **Grammar/Word Choice**:  
   - *“Lead to”* → *“Led to”* (past tense).  
   - *“He can show this by instilling”* → *“They can demonstrate this by instilling”* (gender-neutral language).  
3. **Conciseness**: Trim redundant phrases (e.g., *“as far as operations are concerned”* → *“in operations”*).  

**STRUCTURE IMPROVEMENTS:**  
1. **Thesis Clarity**: The introduction should explicitly state the essay’s central claim (e.g., *“HR managers ensure quality by fostering a culture of excellence, bridging communication gaps, and aligning training with organizational goals”*).  
2. **Paragraph Flow**: Use transition words (*“Furthermore,” “Consequently”*) to connect ideas between paragraphs (e.g., after discussing training, link to how it reduces defects).  
3. **Conclusion**: Expand beyond summary—reiterate the HR manager’s strategic importance and suggest future trends (e.g., digital tools for quality monitoring).
