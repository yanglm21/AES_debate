1. **Tone Consistency**: The tone shifts between formal business analysis and more casual observations. For example, phrases like "IBM is very good at meeting the needs" could be strengthened to "IBM consistently meets client demands" for a more professional tone.

2. **Word Choice**: Some terms are unnecessarily repetitive ("critical provision" appears 9 times) or imprecise ("technological burst through" should be "breakthrough"). Vary your vocabulary and use more specific technical terms where appropriate.

3. **Sentence Structure**: Many sentences are overly long and complex, which can obscure meaning. For example: "The presence of these key players...makes the competition intense for IBM" could be simplified to "These competitors intensify market pressure on IBM."

4. **Citation Overuse**: The frequent (Williams, 76) citations disrupt the flow. Consider synthesizing multiple points into single citations where possible.

5. **Clarity Issues**: Some statements are vague, like "This is how the company has attained its competitive advantages." Specify which strategies or factors contributed.

For stronger business writing:
- Use more active voice ("IBM dominates" vs "IBM is among")
- Tighten transitions between sections
- Replace colloquial phrases with professional equivalents
- Ensure parallel structure in lists and comparisons

The content is thorough, but refining these language elements would make your analysis more polished and persuasive.
